Same budget. Best lead weeks of the year.

The journey

Not more budget – a different allocation

01 · The challenge

More leads without a bigger budget

The client ran paid media on Google and Meta, with organic search driving half of all site traffic. The question wasn’t how much to spend, but where in the funnel the money would work hardest.

02 · What we did

Weighting toward Meta top-of-funnel

Total spend held steady, but a larger share went to a Meta campaign optimised for landing page views: low-cost, relevant visits rather than direct conversions.

03 · The result

The three strongest weeks of the year

Weeks 22–24 were the best of the year, with average weekly lead volume 55% above the comparison baseline in weeks 23–24, and the best week 69% above it. More than 60% of leads were qualified.

The numbers

Leads rose when top-of-funnel got more room

What we learned

Top-of-funnel isn’t waste – it fills the funnel

  1. Measure top-of-funnel on what it creates

    Not last-click leads, but traffic, engagement and the demand that shows up later in search and retargeting.

  2. Reallocate before you increase

    The same spend produced the best lead weeks of the year because the money moved up the funnel.

  3. The effect is delayed

    When top-of-funnel is switched off, leads don’t drop overnight. The funnel slowly runs dry.

  4. See every channel in one cockpit

    Leads, Meta spend and traffic side by side. Otherwise top-of-funnel looks like waste and gets cut.

The method

Precision Creative Engine

This case is a textbook example of the dual-track principle in our Precision Creative Engine: top-of-funnel and bottom-of-funnel do different jobs and must be measured differently.

We combine an audit-first view of creative and channels with a dedicated test budget and a cockpit dashboard, so decisions are made on the whole funnel – not just last click.

Results were observed over the period and are not from a controlled experiment. A holdout test is the natural next step to isolate the effect.
